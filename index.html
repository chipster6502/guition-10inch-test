MiSTer Monitor — Guition 10.1" test build (ESP32-P4 v3)

This build is for boards with the newer ESP32-P4 v3.x chip. On those, the released firmware restarts in a loop right after flashing and prints nothing after entry 0x4ff29ed0: it was built for the earlier chip revision, and the two are not binary-compatible. You can confirm your revision in the flashing log (Chip is ESP32-P4 (revision v3.x)) or in the first boot line (ESP-ROM:esp32p4-eco7).

Not for older boards: on a v1.x chip this build will not start. Those keep using the normal flasher page.

Do the SD card steps first — the firmware needs them on its very first boot.

1. Prepare the SD card

  1. Put the card in your computer and download c6_firmware.bin into its root, next to config.ini.
  2. Open config.ini and add this line in the [ui] section:
    panel_rev=v2

The batch number on the sticker at the back tells you which panel you have — it is the figure in parentheses after the SKU. Batch 2624 and later need v2; earlier ones need v1.

2. Flash

  1. Use a computer (not a phone) with Chrome or Edge.
  2. Connect the board with a USB-C data cable (not a charge-only one) to the port right next to the small white connector. It is labelled USB UART inside the case.
  3. Click Install / Connect, pick the port and wait without touching anything.

Firefox and Safari won't work (they lack Web Serial).

If no port shows up or you get "Failed to initialize": check that the cable is in the port next to the white connector. The other two USB-C ports power the board too, but the flasher cannot connect through them. If the flasher still fails on the right port, esptool --chip esp32p4 write-flash 0x0 guition-10inch-test.bin does the same job.

3. What should happen

Put the card back in, power the display and watch the first boot. It checks the WiFi coprocessor, finds it out of date, updates it from c6_firmware.bin with a progress bar, and restarts itself once. Do not power it off during that bar. After the restart it should connect to WiFi normally, and the screen should show the interface rather than banding.

Either way, please post the serial log from power-on to the issue (115200 baud, same USB-C port). The useful parts are the first ESP-ROM lines and the lines starting with [C6] and [DISPLAY]. If it still restarts in a loop, a few cycles of the log are enough.
